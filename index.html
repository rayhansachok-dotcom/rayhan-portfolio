IV
Chapter Four

From Idea to Impact

LCC Day 2025 · Designing for Dignity

The problem was human: persons with disabilities often face barriers to independent meal preparation. We set out to solve this with empathy-first design, building a device that restores dignity and independence. The solution required us to think like engineers, lead with compassion, and fail our way to something that actually worked.

Problem
Persons with disabilities face real barriers to independent meal preparation, we identified this as a problem worth solving.
Research
Understanding existing solutions, their limitations, and what users actually needed.
Design
Sketching, debating, refining, learning that good design is mostly good listening.
Prototype
Building the first version, cardboard, components, and a lot of optimism.
Testing & Reflection
Each failure made the next version better. The process taught me more than the outcome ever could.

Industry Exposure & Mentorship

Learning from the Best

One of my greatest inspirations is my godfather, an alumnus of Ngee Ann Polytechnic, who went on to own a company specialising in industrial seamless flooring solutions. His company has successfully delivered projects for globally recognised names including Rolls-Royce Aviation and Bombardier Aviation.

Seeing the trust that world-renowned companies place in his work has shown me the importance of professionalism, consistency, and delivering quality at the highest standard. His journey has inspired me to dream bigger and work towards building my own successful business.

One day, I hope to own a business that reflects the same dedication and excellence, while creating an even greater impact through innovation, leadership, and continuous growth.

Lesson One

Lesson Two

Lesson Three
